# Self-Attention Mechanism (QKV Matrices)

In the vanilla self-attention mechanism of [[Transformers]], the input sequence is projected into three distinct matrices: **Query (Q)**, **Key (K)**, and **Value (V)**. These matrices are generated by multiplying the input embeddings by three separate learned weight matrices: $W_Q$, $W_K$, and $W_V$.

The attention output is computed as:
$$Attention(Q, K, V) = softmax\left(\frac{QK^T}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right)V$$

A critical architectural design of self-attention is that **these three matrices do not share weights**, and the attention scores are scaled by $\sqrt{d_k}$.

## Scaled Dot-Product Attention: Why divide by $\sqrt{d_k}$?

In the self-attention equation, $d_k$ represents the **dimensionality of the Key vectors** (and Query vectors). For example, in a Transformer with a hidden dimension of 512 and 8 attention heads, the dimensionality of each head's key vectors is $d_k = 512 / 8 = 64$.

The division by $\sqrt{d_k}$ is critical for training stability to prevent the **vanishing gradient problem**. 
As the dimensionality $d_k$ increases, the variance of the dot product between the query and key vectors grows linearly with it. If the components of the vectors are independent random variables with a variance of 1, the variance of their dot product becomes $d_k$. 

When these large-variance dot products are fed into the softmax function, the softmax outputs become extremely "sharp" (close to 1 for the maximum value and 0 for others), pushing the function into regions where its gradients are exceedingly small. 

By dividing the dot product by the standard deviation ($\sqrt{d_k}$), the variance is scaled back down to $1$. This standardizes the inputs to the softmax function, ensuring stable gradients and effective learning regardless of the vector dimensionality.

## Why $W_Q$ and $W_K$ Must Not Share Weights

### 1. Breaking Symmetry (Directionality)
Self-attention needs to be highly asymmetric because language and relationships are directional. 
* **Query (Q)** represents: *"What information am I looking for?"* (from the perspective of the current token).
* **Key (K)** represents: *"What information do I contain?"* (from the perspective of the token being looked at).

If $W_Q = W_K = W$, the unnormalized attention score between token $i$ and token $j$ would be:
$$Score(i, j) = (x_i W)(x_j W)^T = x_i W W^T x_j^T$$

Because $W W^T$ is symmetric, the attention score $Score(i, j)$ would always equal $Score(j, i)$. In language, this is fundamentally incorrect (e.g., in "The cat", "The" must attend strongly to "cat", but "cat" does not need to attend strongly to "The"). Separate matrices allow the model to learn a non-symmetric compatibility matrix.

### 2. The Collapse of the Transformer (Identity Matrix Degeneration)
From a mathematical perspective, if $W_Q$ equals $W_K$, the network develops a massive bias where **every token overwhelmingly attends to itself**.

Let $y_i = x_i W$ be the projected vector. The attention score between token $i$ and token $j$ is the dot product $y_i \cdot y_j$.
The attention score of token $i$ to *itself* is $y_i \cdot y_i = \|y_i\|^2$.

Due to the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality ($a \cdot b = \|a\| \|b\| \cos \theta$), the dot product is heavily dependent on the angle between the vectors. When a token attends to itself, the angle is 0, making $\cos(0) = 1$. Assuming Layer Normalization keeps vector magnitudes similar, this guarantees that $y_i \cdot y_i$ will almost always be the maximum possible score in its row.

This causes a catastrophic failure chain:
1. **Identity Matrix Degeneration:** After applying the Softmax function, the attention matrix degrades into something resembling an Identity Matrix (1s on the diagonal, ~0s elsewhere).
2. **Failure of Context Mixing:** The purpose of attention is to gather context from *other* tokens. If the attention matrix is an identity matrix, token $i$ only extracts information from token $i$, effectively turning the attention layer into a redundant feed-forward layer. Information stops flowing across the sequence.
3. **The Representation Paradox:** To force token A to attend to token B, the model would be forced to make their embeddings nearly identical ($x_A \approx x_B$). But doing so would destroy their individual meanings.

Separate $W_Q$ and $W_K$ solve this by allowing $x_A W_Q$ to perfectly align with $x_B W_K$ (resulting in a high attention score) **without** requiring the actual embedding $x_A$ to look anything like $x_B$.

## Why $W_V$ is Separate (Matching vs. Extracting)

While Q and K are used to calculate the *attention scores* (the routing/matching phase), the **Value (V)** represents the actual *content* that is extracted and aggregated.

* **Value (V)** represents: *"If a token attends to me, what actual information do I send them?"*

The features needed to figure out *whether* two words should connect are often very different from the features needed to *understand* the sentence once they are connected. For instance, matching a pronoun to a noun requires Q and K to focus on gender and plurality. But once matched, the V matrix must transmit the actual semantic meaning of the noun into the pronoun's representation.

**Analogy:**
* **Query ($W_Q$):** The search terms typed into a library computer.
* **Key ($W_K$):** The title and keywords printed on the spine of the book.
* **Value ($W_V$):** The actual text inside the book that you read. 
These three require entirely different representations of the underlying data.
